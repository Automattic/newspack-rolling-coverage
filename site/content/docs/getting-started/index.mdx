By the end of this page, you will know what Rolling Coverage does and what makes it a powerful option for liveblogging.

Rolling Coverage is for stories that keep developing: a storm, an election night, a match, a court case. Editors post short updates as the story moves. Readers see each update appear on the page as it's published, without reloading.

Updates can come from the WordPress dashboard or from a Slack channel. The feed can sit on one main page, on the homepage, in a sidebar, or in all three at once.

## Rolling Coverage's liveblogging vocab words

- **Coverage**: One developing story, such as "Storm Ingrid". Every update belongs to a liveblog.
- **Entry**: One update in a liveblog. An entry has a time, an optional title, and any content a post can have: text, images, video, or embeds.
- **Status**: Where a coverage stands. **Live** means updates are coming in. **Paused** means the story is quiet for now. **Ended** means the story is over. A site can rename these three in its settings.
- **Layout**: How the liveblog looks on a page. Rolling Coverage comes with 13 layouts, from a full live blog to a short strip for a homepage.
- **Pinned entry**: An entry kept at the top of the feed, above newer ones. Use it for the most important update, such as an evacuation order.
- **Ended coverage**: A liveblog whose status is **Ended**. Feeds stop checking for new entries, and no new entries can be added.

## Roles and permissions

Anyone who can write posts on the site can add entries. Creating a liveblog requires an Editor or Administrator. Connecting Slack and setting up AI key takeaways requires an Administrator.
